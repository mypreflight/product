# Business model

Pricing, what's free, what's paid, and the rules money never breaks.

Last reviewed: 2026-10-20

## Model

- Free during the beta.
- Later a licence-based SaaS. **Today's features stay free.** Future depth features are paid:
  - cargo history and aircraft history;
  - deeper cargo context, e.g. salmon with a 12-hour cold-chain limit on an 8-hour flight, and what a delay does to it;
  - plain-language NOTAMs.
- Price: a symbolic **€5/month or €50/year** (two months free). A buy-me-a-coffee link alone was rejected: it isn't stable revenue.
- Payments: Stripe, the simplest first integration.

## Goal

Cover infrastructure and development costs and break even. A community around the product is the real bonus. Not a profit-driven project.

Hopes for the first six months: break even on infrastructure, ideally with enough left to fund some paid ads (e.g. Facebook). Above all: active, engaged users who give feedback, whether they pay or not.

## Spending principle

A self-funded project. Get the most out of free sources first. Spend money, even low hundreds, only with very good reasoning.

## Rules money never breaks

- No ads.
- No pay-to-win: paid features never affect score.
- Sponsorship is welcome from add-on developers: sponsored events, city pairs, or bonus points for flying their new aircraft (detectable from sim data). Always by aircraft **type**, so free alternatives qualify and nobody is forced to buy.

## Team

Solo for now; Oskar works as product owner and codes less thanks to agentic development. A team of 5 to 8 community volunteers is expected, people who feel the product is made for them. Paid hires only if revenue ever allows, which is unlikely.
